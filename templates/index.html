Understanding the Disease

Why Does Parkinson's Disease Occur?

Parkinson's disease results from the gradual loss of dopamine-producing neurons in the brain. While the exact cause remains unknown, several factors play a critical role.

🧬

Genetic Factors

Specific gene mutations (LRRK2, PARK7, SNCA, PINK1) have been identified in families with a history of PD. About 10–15% of cases have a genetic link, making family history a significant risk indicator.

🏭

Environmental Toxins

Prolonged exposure to pesticides (paraquat, rotenone), herbicides, and industrial chemicals significantly increases risk. Rural populations and agricultural workers show higher PD prevalence.

🧪

Dopamine Depletion

The brain's substantia nigra progressively loses neurons that produce dopamine — a chemical essential for smooth, coordinated muscle movement. Symptoms appear when 60–80% of these neurons are lost.

👴

Age & Oxidative Stress

Risk increases sharply after age 60. Ageing leads to accumulated oxidative damage in brain cells, mitochondrial dysfunction, and protein misfolding (α-synuclein / Lewy bodies).

Recognising the Signs

Symptoms of Parkinson's Disease

PD symptoms develop gradually over years. Early non-motor symptoms often appear 5–10 years before visible tremors.

🫳

Resting Tremor

Involuntary shaking of hands, fingers, or jaw — most noticeable at rest and typically starts on one side of the body.

Motor
🦿

Muscle Rigidity

Stiffness in limbs and trunk causing reduced range of motion, pain, and difficulty with everyday tasks like dressing.

Motor
🚶

Bradykinesia

Slowness of movement — walking becomes shuffling, facial expressions diminish ("mask face"), and tasks take longer.

Motor
⚖️

Postural Instability

Impaired balance and coordination, leading to frequent falls. Appears in later stages and significantly impacts independence.

Motor
🗣️

Voice Changes

Speech becomes soft, monotone, or slurred. Voice biomarkers like jitter and shimmer change — the basis of our AI detection system.

Biomarker
😴

Sleep & Mood Issues

REM sleep disorder, insomnia, depression, anxiety, and loss of smell. These non-motor symptoms can precede diagnosis by years.

Non-Motor
Global & National Impact

Parkinson's Disease Around the World

PD is the fastest-growing neurological disorder worldwide. Understanding its scale highlights the urgent need for early detection tools.

A Growing Global Health Crisis

Parkinson's disease affects over 10 million people worldwide and is projected to double by 2040 due to ageing populations. It is the 2nd most common neurodegenerative disorder after Alzheimer's.

Men are 1.5× more likely to develop PD than women. The economic burden exceeds $52 billion annually in the United States alone, including medical costs and lost productivity.

Impact in India

India has the 3rd highest number of Parkinson's patients in the world. With a population exceeding 1.4 billion, an estimated 7–10 lakh (0.7–1 million) people live with PD. The prevalence is rising rapidly due to increasing life expectancy, pesticide exposure in rural areas, and limited access to neurological care.
